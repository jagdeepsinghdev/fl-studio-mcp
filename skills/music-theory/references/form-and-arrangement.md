# Arrangement — form and energy

FL mechanics for sections (one pattern per section, markers) live in `fl-patterns` →
`references/sections.md`. This page is how to decide what goes where.

## The energy curve
A track is a plan for energy over time. Draw it before arranging: low intro → high hook →
lower verse → high hook → peak (final hook) → release (outro). Energy comes from:
- **density** (number of layers playing),
- **register** (adding lows or highs widens the sound),
- **rhythmic activity** (1/8 → 1/16 hats, more kick hits),
- **loudness/brightness** (filter opening, saturation).

## The 4/8-bar change rule
- Listeners expect something to change **every 4 bars** (small: a hat variation, an 808
  turnaround, a melody tweak) and **every 8 bars** (bigger: a layer in or out).
- **Every 16 bars** = a new section.
- Never let the exact same 4 bars play more than ~4 times in a row without at least a fill.

## Common forms (bars; convention, not auditioned)

**Type beat / rap instrumental (leaves room for a rapper):**
| Section | Bars | What plays |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 4-8 | melody alone or filtered, maybe a riser/tag |
| Hook | 8-16 | everything: full drums, 808, melody |
| Verse | 16 | melody thinner or filtered, hats busier, 808 simpler (space for words) |
| Hook | 8-16 | everything |
| Verse 2 | 16 | variation: a new counter-melody, drum switch-up |
| Hook | 8-16 | everything, maybe one extra layer |
| Outro | 4-8 | melody alone, fade/filter out |
Total about 2:30-3:30.

**Pop / R&B:** intro 4 → verse 8-16 → pre-chorus 4-8 (build) → chorus 8 → verse → pre →
chorus → bridge 8 (new chords) → final chorus (biggest) → outro.

**A A B A B C (attention blueprint):** verse → verse → chorus → verse → chorus → bridge (→ final
chorus). Repetition keeps it familiar; the chorus and then the bridge arrive just as the repeat would
get boring. Why, and the research behind it: `hit-song-patterns.md` §4.

**EDM / house:** intro 16-32 (DJ friendly) → build 8-16 → drop 16-32 → breakdown 16 → build →
drop 2 → outro 16-32.

**Loop beat (minimal, e.g. a one-chord trap loop):** the same 4-bar loop throughout; the
arrangement is *only* additions and subtractions. That's why such beats rely on 808
turnarounds and hat variation.

## Addition and subtraction (the producer's main tool)
Start from the full hook loop, then **remove** parts to make the other sections:
- Intro = hook minus drums minus 808.
- Verse = hook minus a melody layer (or melody filtered) with a simpler 808.
- Break = hook minus drums (or minus the 808) for 2-4 bars.
Subtraction keeps everything coherent: every section is made from the same material.

## Transitions (the last bar before a new section)
- **Drop-out:** silence the drums (or everything but the melody) for the last 1-2 beats; the
  return hits harder. The most effective trap transition.
- **Hat roll / snare roll** into beat 1.
- **808 slide** up or down into the new section's root.
- **Riser / reverse cymbal** (white-noise sweep) over 1-4 bars into a hook or drop.
- **Filter sweep:** low-pass the melody in the intro, open it at the hook.
- **Impact** on beat 1 of the hook (a crash, a boom, a reversed reverb tail ending on 1).
- **Unexpected ending:** cut the song off a little early, so the listener replays it
  (`fl-mixing` `background-vocals.md` §5).
- **Producer tag:** in the intro's first 1-2 bars (type-beat convention).

- **Anti-drop:** build with sweeps as if a big drop is coming, then land on something smaller or
  groovier (`idea-to-record.md` §5).

## Variation without new material
- **Every chorus a little different:** one new thing per chorus (a pitched-up cowbell, one more vocal
  stack), never a new melody (`idea-to-record.md` §6).
- Flip the melody up an octave in the second hook.
- Reverse one sample for a bar.
- Change the hat grid (1/8 → 1/16 → triplets) between verse and hook.
- Mute the kick for a bar so the 808 rings alone.
- Pitch the whole melody down an octave or half-speed for a bridge.

## Arranging around a vocalist (beats for rappers/singers)
- The **verse is the vocalist's space:** fewer mid-range layers (300 Hz-3 kHz is where the
  voice lives). Keep the melody sparse or filtered.
- The **hook can be fuller**, since the vocal is usually doubled/stacked there.
- Leave the **first beat of a bar** clean in the verse sometimes, where rappers start phrases.

## Classical and song forms (the professional vocabulary)

| Form | Shape | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Strophic** | A A A … | same music, new lyrics each verse: folk, hymns, many bhajans |
| **Binary** | A B (each often repeated: \|\|: A :\|\|: B :\|\|) | baroque dances; A modulates to V (or III in minor), B returns home |
| **Rounded binary** | A B A′ | B is short, then part of A returns |
| **Ternary** | A B A | contrast in the middle (new key or mode); da capo arias, minuets with trio |
| **32-bar song (AABA)** | A8 A8 B8 (bridge/"middle eight") A8 | Tin Pan Alley, jazz standards, Beatles |
| **Verse–chorus** | V C V C B C | modern pop; the chorus holds the title and the highest energy |
| **12-bar blues** | AAB lyric over the 12-bar progression | `harmony.md` §3 |
| **Rondo** | A B A C A (B A) | a recurring refrain: like a hook returning between verses |
| **Theme and variations** | A A′ A″ A‴ … | same harmony/melody, varied rhythm, mode, texture: the loop-based producer's form |
| **Sonata form** | exposition (theme 1 in I, transition, theme 2 in V or III) → development (fragmentation, modulation) → recapitulation (both themes in I) → coda | the large-scale tension-release model: build, wander, return |
| **Through-composed** | no repeats | film cues, art songs |
| **Sthayi–antara** (Indian) | sthayi (refrain, lower/middle register) ↔ antara (verse, higher register, reaching taar Sa) | Bollywood songs: the mukhda (opening hook line) returns after each antara |

### Phrase-level forms
- **Period:** antecedent (ends on a half cadence) + consequent (ends on a full cadence): question → answer.
- **Sentence** (Schoenberg): presentation (basic idea + its repeat, 2+2 bars) → continuation
  (fragmentation, acceleration, 4 bars) → cadence. Very common in pop hooks and in 8-bar trap melodies.
- **Hook / title placement:** the title on the strongest bar (bar 1 or the last bar of the chorus), at the highest or most rhythmically distinct moment.

### Pop-song specifics
- **Pre-chorus** builds (rising melody, faster harmonic rhythm, drums thin out or build) into the chorus.
- **Post-chorus / drop**: an instrumental or vocal-chop hook after the chorus (EDM-pop).
- **Bridge:** new chords (often starting on IV or vi, or a borrowed chord), new melody, a different lyric angle; often 8 bars before the last chorus.
- **Last chorus lift:** a key change (truck-driver), a double chorus, ad-libs, extra layers, or a drop-out and slam back.
