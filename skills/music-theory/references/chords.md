# Chords — construction, symbols, inversions, extensions, voicings

## 1. Triads (stacked thirds)

| Quality | Formula (semitones) | Intervals | Symbol | Roman | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Major | 0 4 7 | M3 + m3 | C | I | stable, bright |
| Minor | 0 3 7 | m3 + M3 | Cm, C-, Cmin | i | stable, dark |
| Diminished | 0 3 6 | m3 + m3 | C°, Cdim | vii° | unstable, tense |
| Augmented | 0 4 8 | M3 + M3 | C+, Caug | III+ | unstable, dreamy, rising |

## 2. Seventh chords

| Quality | Formula | Symbol(s) | Roman (where it's diatonic) | Sound / function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Major 7 | 0 4 7 11 | Cmaj7, CΔ7, CM7 | Imaj7, IVmaj7 | lush, dreamy, R&B, lo-fi |
| Dominant 7 | 0 4 7 10 | C7 | V7 | tension that resolves down a 5th; blues tonic |
| Minor 7 | 0 3 7 10 | Cm7, C-7 | ii7, iii7, vi7; i7 (Dorian/Aeolian) | soulful, relaxed |
| Half-diminished | 0 3 6 10 | Cm7b5, Cø7 | viiø7 (major), iiø7 (minor) | yearning; minor ii-V |
| Fully diminished | 0 3 6 9 | C°7, Cdim7 | vii°7 (harmonic minor) | maximum tension; symmetric (any note is the root) |
| Minor-major 7 | 0 3 7 11 | Cm(maj7), C-Δ7 | i(maj7) (harmonic/melodic minor) | noir, Bond theme, spy film |
| Augmented major 7 | 0 4 8 11 | Cmaj7#5, C+Δ7 | III+maj7 (harmonic minor) | otherworldly |
| Augmented 7 | 0 4 8 10 | C7#5, C+7 | V7#5 | dominant with a rising 5th |
| Dominant 7 sus4 | 0 5 7 10 | C7sus4 | V7sus | modern jazz/gospel, "floating V" |
| Major 6 | 0 4 7 9 | C6 | I6 (not inversion!) | vintage, swing-era tonic |
| Minor 6 | 0 3 7 9 | Cm6 | i6 (Dorian/melodic minor) | jazz minor tonic, noir |

**Notation trap:** a figure "6" after a Roman numeral (I6) means **first inversion** in classical
analysis; "C6" in a chord symbol means **added 6th**. Context decides.

## 3. Extensions and alterations

Extensions continue stacking thirds: 9 (= 2), 11 (= 4), 13 (= 6), an octave up.

| Symbol | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cmaj9 | 0 4 7 11 14 | the R&B/neo-soul tonic |
| C9 | 0 4 7 10 14 | funk dominant |
| Cm9 | 0 3 7 10 14 | lo-fi / neo-soul minor |
| Cm11 | 0 3 7 10 14 17 | modal, quartal-sounding minor (drop the 5th) |
| C11 | 0 (4) 7 10 14 17 | usually voiced without the 3rd = C9sus4 = Bb/C |
| Cmaj7#11 | 0 4 7 11 18 | Lydian tonic; film "wonder" |
| C13 | 0 4 7 10 14 (17) 21 | big-band dominant; omit the 11 (clashes with the 3) |
| Cm13 | 0 3 7 10 14 17 21 | Dorian minor (♮13) |
| Cadd9 / Cadd2 | 0 4 7 14 (or 2) | triad + 9th, no 7th: pop, worship, open |
| C6/9 | 0 4 7 9 14 | pentatonic chord: bossa, jazz tonic, ending chord |
| C7b9 | 0 4 7 10 13 | dominant in minor keys; resolves to a minor i |
| C7#9 | 0 4 7 10 15 | the "Hendrix chord": blues-rock, funk |
| C7#11 | 0 4 7 10 18 | Lydian dominant; tritone sub colour |
| C7b13 | 0 4 7 10 20 | minor-key dominant |
| C7alt | 0 4 10 + b9/#9/#11(b5)/b13(#5) | max-tension V before a resolution; voiced e.g. C E Bb Eb Ab |

**Avoid-note logic:** the natural 11 over a major/dominant chord (a half step over the 3rd) is
usually omitted or raised (#11). The b9 over a minor chord clashes. The natural 13 over minor
is Dorian (fine); b13 over minor is Aeolian (darker).

## 4. Slash chords, polychords, hybrid and quartal chords

- **Slash chord** `X/Y`: chord X over bass note Y.
  - Inversion: C/E (3rd in bass), C/G (5th in bass).
  - Non-chord bass: **C/D** (= D9sus4-ish, the "gospel/fusion sus"), **Fmaj7/G** or **F/G** (= G11:
    the soft pop/gospel dominant), **Ab/C** (minor-key colour), **D/C** (Lydian: C bass + D triad = #11).
- **Hybrid chords:** a triad over a non-chord bass (F/G, Bb/C, Eb/F): the modern "sus
  dominant" of pop, gospel and film. They resolve like a V without the leading tone's pull.
- **Polychords** `D/C` written as a fraction `D over C`: two triads stacked (Stravinsky's
  Petrushka chord C + F#; film brass).
- **Quartal / quintal:** stacked 4ths (C F Bb Eb) or 5ths. Ambiguous, modern, open: McCoy Tyner,
  "So What" voicing (E A D G B), Hans Zimmer, modern R&B pads.
- **Cluster:** adjacent seconds (C D E F); Cowell, film tension, ambient pads.
- **Upper-structure triads (jazz):** a triad on top of a dominant's 3rd-7th shell, e.g. D/C7
  (gives 9 #11 13), Ab/C7 (gives #9 b13 = alt). The pro way to voice extensions cleanly.

## 5. Inversions and figured bass

| Position | Bass | Triad figure | 7th-chord figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Root | root | 5/3 (none) | 7 |
| 1st inversion | 3rd | 6 (6/3) | 6/5 |
| 2nd inversion | 5th | 6/4 | 4/3 |
| 3rd inversion | 7th | — | 4/2 (or 2) |

- **Root position** = strongest, final.
- **1st inversion** = lighter, smooth bass lines (the bass moves by step: I – V6 – vi).
- **2nd inversion (6/4)** is used in specific ways in common practice: **cadential 6/4**
  (I6/4–V: really a V with suspensions), **passing 6/4**, **pedal (neighbour) 6/4**, **arpeggiated 6/4**.
- **3rd inversion (4/2):** the 7th in the bass must fall by step (V4/2 → I6).

## 7. Voicing (how professionals place the notes)

### Principles
1. **Bass = root (or chosen inversion), alone in the low register.** Below ~C3 (130 Hz) keep only
   octaves and fifths; thirds there = mud ("low interval limits": m3 below ~Eb3, M3 below ~Bb2,
   P5 below ~Bb1 sound muddy).
2. **Guide tones** (3rd and 7th) define the chord; the root and 5th are optional when a bass plays.
3. **Top note = melody**: choose voicings so the top voice moves smoothly.
4. **Voice leading:** keep common tones; move other voices by the smallest step
   (`voice-leading-and-counterpoint.md`).

### Standard voicing types
| Type | How | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Close position | all voices within an octave | pads, simple piano |
| Open / spread | 1-2 voices moved up an octave | strings, choirs, wide pads |
| **Shell** (jazz) | root + 3 + 7 (or root + 7 + 3) | left-hand comping, guitar |
| **Rootless A/B** (Bill Evans) | 3-5-7-9 / 7-9-3-5 over a bass player | jazz piano, neo-soul keys |
| **Drop 2** | close voicing, 2nd-from-top note dropped an octave | jazz guitar, horn sections |
| **Drop 3**, drop 2+4 | likewise, other voices dropped | big-band, guitar |
| **So What / quartal** | stacked 4ths + a M3 on top | modal jazz, modern pads |
| **Upper structure** | triad over a 3-7 shell | altered dominants |
| **Gospel / R&B stacks** | 1-5-1 left hand; 3-7-9 or 7-9-3-5 right; frequent passing chords | gospel, R&B, neo-soul |
| **Power / 5ths** | root + 5th (+ octave) | rock, trap 808+5th, film low brass |

### Instrument-specific
- **Piano:** left hand roots/shells or 10ths, right hand 3-4 note voicings; avoid low clusters.
- **Guitar:** CAGED shapes, triads on string sets, drop-2/3; open strings for ringing colour.
- **Pads/strings:** spread voicings, legato voice leading, avoid doubling the 3rd.
- **Bells/plucks (trap):** arpeggiate the chord or use 2-3 notes (3rd + 7th, 5th + 9th) high up.
- **Choir (SATB):** soprano melody; doubled root preferred; never double the leading tone.
