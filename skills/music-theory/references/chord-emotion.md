# Chord emotion — pick a mood, pick a progression, then voice it for that mood

Use this when the question is "what chords feel X?" or "how do I make this progression
sound sadder / happier / darker?". Symbols and voicing mechanics live in `chords.md` (§6 diatonic
7ths, §7 voicing); cadences and borrowed chords in `harmony.md`; dark loops in `harmony.md` §11.
This file only adds the mood layer. Concepts are general theory, not a transcription of any video.

## 1. The levers (what makes a progression feel the way it does)

| Lever | Pushes the feel toward |
|---|---|
| Major tonic, bright thirds on top | happy, open, joyful |
| Minor tonic, more minor chords than major | dark, serious, introspective |
| Descending bass or chord roots (1 → 7 → 6 → 5) | sad, heavy, wistful |
| Long held chords (half or whole notes) | sad, spacious, cinematic |
| Sevenths and ninths on some chords | groove, warmth, nostalgia |
| Sevenths on *every* chord | blurs the emotion; use sparingly |
| Sus2 / sus4 (no 3rd) | open, ambiguous, floating |
| Pedal tone under changing chords | cohesion, dreaminess, tension without leaving home |
| Tonic pedal | home, reassurance, calm |
| Dominant pedal | build-up, anticipation |
| Diminished chord on the final beat | unstable, unresolved, usually avoided in pop |
| Major chord on the same root (Picardy-style lift) | sudden hope in a minor piece |

Rule of thumb: pick **one** primary lever per mood and let the rest support it. Mixing
three strong levers gives a muddy mood.

## 3. Pivot: move a progression between two moods

Most progressions are one step away from another mood. Keep the bass line and change one thing.

| From → To | Change |
|---|---|
| Sad → hopeful | Move the pedal from the tonic to the mediant (III) or relative-major tonic |
| Sad → heroic | Move the first chord down to the submediant, then return to VI; or swap VII for V |
| Happy → nostalgic | Swap one major chord for its relative minor (IV → iv) |
| Dark → cinematic | Strip the 3rds and 5ths, leave one drone |
| Dreamy → brooding | Replace the ending ii° with a minor iv, lower the bass |
| Joy → lighthearted | Turn the last chord into a dominant seventh |

## 4. Revoicing recipes (same chords, different feel)

These are the moves a producer uses on the same chord list. All are standard voicing practice
(`chords.md` §7 has the general principles).

1. **Drop the roots an octave.** Bass weight → groove, grounding. Keep the top voices in range.
2. **Keep thirds on top.** Thirds carry major/minor colour; keep them audible.
3. **Pull the fifths down an octave** when the mood needs warmth and less shine.
4. **Add a tonic pedal** on a new track or a held low note. Use it when chord changes are large.
5. **Sevenths on one or two chords only.** Keep the others as triads so the 7ths land as events.
6. **Sus2 on one chord** (drop the 3rd, add the 2nd) to add instability without changing function.
7. **Double the root in the bass** for sadness and weight; leave the bass single for airiness.
8. **Shorten the last chord** or push one chord onto the upbeat to add lift.
9. **Vary the rhythm of the same voicing** (held vs. stabbed). Rhythm is often more emotional than the chords.

## 5. Instrumentation supports the mood

- Sad / melancholic: strings, cellos, soft piano, slow attack pads, reverb tails.
- Happy / joyful: supersaw, bright bell or plucked lead, fast 16th arpeggios.
- Dark: sub bass, low drones, detuned pads, minimal melody.
- Groove: electric piano, clavinet, muted guitar, tight 808 or bass, offbeat chord stabs.

See `timbre-and-orchestration.md` for ranges and colour choices.

## 6. Quick check before you commit to a progression

1. Which lever is the main one? (§1)
2. Does the bass move down (sad) or up (bright)?
3. Are there more minor chords than major? (dark)
4. Is there a pedal, and is it the tonic? (home)
5. Did I use sevenths on every chord? (if yes, cut some)
6. Is the last chord diminished? (if yes, consider a major or minor neighbour)
