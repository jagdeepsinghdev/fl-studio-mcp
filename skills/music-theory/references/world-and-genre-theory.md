# Genre and world theory — the musical DNA of each style

FL build recipes (BPM, drum steps, presets) live in `fl-genres/references/<genre>.md`. This
page is the *theory* of each style: harmony, melody, rhythm and sound, so you can write in a
genre rather than copy a template. Values are conventions (not auditioned); listen to references.

## 1. Hip-hop family

| Style | Harmony | Melody | Rhythm | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Trap** (Atlanta; Future, Metro Boomin, Southside) | minor loops: i–VI, i–VI–VII, drones; Phrygian/harmonic-minor colour | sparse bells/flutes/plucks, short motifs, b6→5 and b2→1 sighs | 130-160 half-time; snare on 3; 1/8-1/16 hats with triplet/32nd rolls; syncopated kick+808 | distorted 808 with slides, dark pads, choirs |
| **Drill** (UK/NY) | harmonic minor, Phrygian dominant, Andalusian loops | eerie piano/strings, tremolo, chromatic lines | 138-145; snares displaced (often step 9 + a late one), triplet hats, sliding 808 that changes notes often | gliding 808, horror strings |
| **Boom-bap** ('90s NY) | soul/jazz samples: m7, m9, maj7, dominant 9 | sample chops, horn stabs | 85-95, swung 16ths, snare on 2 & 4, ghost notes | dusty drums, upright bass, vinyl |
| **Lo-fi hip-hop** | ii9–V13–Imaj9, Dorian m9/m11, chromatic slides | simple jazzy keys, guitar | 70-90, heavy swing, lazy snare | tape wow/flutter, low-pass, vinyl crackle |
| **West Coast / G-funk** | Mixolydian/Dorian, m7–IV7 | portamento synth leads (Moog), talk box | 90-100, funk groove | whiny lead, deep bass |
| **Rage / plugg** | major or minor triad loops, very few chords | arpeggiated synths, bright detuned leads (rage) or soft bells (plugg) | 140-160 | supersaws, distorted 808s |

## 2. R&B, soul, pop

| Style | Harmony | Melody | Rhythm |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contemporary R&B** | maj9/m9/m11, IVmaj7–iii7–vi7, slash chords, borrowed iv | melismatic vocals, pentatonic runs | 60-100, swung 16ths, sparse drums |
| **Neo-soul** | extended + quartal chords, chromatic planing, modal interchange | behind-the-beat phrasing | late, "drunk" (J Dilla) timing |
| **Gospel** | passing diminished chords, IV/V, tritone subs, walk-ups, key changes | call and response, runs | shuffle or 6/8; organ + drums |
| **Pop** | I–V–vi–IV family, vi–IV–I–V, borrowed iv or bVII for colour | stepwise, singable hooks, repetition; the chorus higher than the verse | 90-130, 4/4 straight |
| **K-pop / J-pop** | royal road IVmaj7–V7–iii7–vi, frequent key changes, secondary dominants | dense melodies, sections in contrast | genre-switching mid-song |

## 3. Electronic

| Style | Harmony | Rhythm | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| **House** | m7/m9 chord stabs, i–VII–VI loops, Dorian | 120-128, four-on-the-floor, off-beat hats, clap 2 & 4 | piano/organ stabs, filtered loops |
| **Techno** | minimal: one note, drones, atonal stabs | 125-140, four-on-the-floor, rolling percussion | modular sequences, rumble kick |
| **Trance** | i–VI–III–VII, epic minor; supersaw chords | 128-140, off-beat bass | arps, gated pads, long breakdowns |
| **Drum and bass** | minor, Dorian (liquid), jazz chords (liquid) | 170-176, breakbeats (Amen), 2-step snare 2 & 4 | reese bass, sub |
| **Dubstep** | minor, Phrygian, power chords | 140 half-time, snare on 3 | wobble/growl bass |
| **Future bass** | maj7/maj9/add9, IV–V–iii–vi, sidechained chords | 130-160 half-time | supersaw chords with pitch-bend, vocal chops |
| **Afro house / amapiano** | minor 7/9 loops, gospel-ish chords (amapiano) | 112-118; log-drum basslines; shakers | log drum, piano chords |

## 4. Latin and Caribbean

| Style | Theory |
|---|---|
| **Reggaeton / dembow** | 88-100; dembow rhythm (kick on 1, 2, 3, 4; snare on the "a" of 1 and the "&" of 2, i.e. 3-3-2 subdivision); minor keys (Aeolian, Phrygian), i–VI–VII / i–iv loops |
| **Clave (Afro-Cuban)** | the 2-bar timeline: **son clave 3-2** = bar 1: beat 1, the "&" of 2, beat 4 · bar 2: beats 2 and 3 (16-step grid: 1, 7, 13 \| 5, 9); rumba clave moves the 3rd hit of the 3-side an 8th late (from beat 4, step 13, to the "&" of 4, step 15). Every part locks to the clave direction |
| **Tresillo** | 3+3+2 eighth-note grouping over a bar (16-step grid: 1, 7, 13; compressed to 16ths over half a bar: 1, 4, 7): the root of dembow, Afrobeats, trap hi-hats, pop syncopation |
| **Bossa nova** | maj7/m7/7(9)(13) chords, chromatic voice leading; guitar pattern based on clave; 130-150 felt in 2 |
| **Salsa** | montuno piano patterns (tumbao bass anticipating the downbeat: the bass avoids beat 1) |
| **Dancehall** | 90-110, riddims built on tresillo-based patterns, minor loops |

## 5. African

| Style | Theory |
|---|---|
| **Afrobeats** (Nigeria/Ghana) | 95-115, tresillo/clave-based percussion, shakers, syncopated kick; major or Dorian/Mixolydian loops; highlife-style guitar |
| **Afrobeat** (Fela) | long modal vamps (Dorian/Mixolydian), interlocking guitar ostinatos, horn riffs, 12/8 feel layers |
| **West African polyrhythm** | 12/8 bell patterns (standard pattern: 7 strokes over 12 pulses), 3:2 cross-rhythms, call and response |
| **Highlife / soukous** | major keys, I–IV–V, interlocking bright guitars |

## 6. South Asian (Indian subcontinent) — see `indian-classical-ragas.md` for the raga system

| Style | Theory |
|---|---|
| **Bollywood / filmi** | raga-based melodies (Yaman, Bhairavi, Kafi, Pahadi, Shivranjani) harmonised with Western chords; orchestral strings; Keherwa/Dadra rhythms |
| **Punjabi / bhangra** | dhol chaal rhythm (8-beat Keherwa feel with a swung, bouncing accent; bass "dagga" strokes on 1 and the "&" of 2 typical), tumbi riffs (high, staccato, pentatonic/Mixolydian), major or Kafi/Dorian; modern Punjabi pop at 90-100 BPM mixes trap 808s with dhol |
| **Ghazal / qawwali** | ghazal: slow, Urdu poetry, harmonium, raga-light melodies (Bhairavi, Yaman, Kafi); qawwali: call and response, handclaps, building tempo (Sufi) |
| **Sufi / devotional** | drone + harmonium, Bhairavi/Kafi/Bhairav, accelerating rhythm |
| **Carnatic** | South Indian classical: 72 melakartas, gamaka-heavy ornamentation, tala system (Adi tala = 8 beats), konnakol rhythm syllables |

## 7. Middle Eastern (maqam)

- **Maqam** = mode + melodic behaviour, built from **ajnas** (3-5-note fragments, singular *jins*).
- Uses **quarter tones** (half-flats, notated with a slashed flat): e.g. **Rast** C D E½b F G A B½b C;
  **Bayati** D E½b F G A Bb C D; **Saba** D E½b F Gb A Bb C; **Hijaz** D Eb F# G A Bb C D (≈ Phrygian dominant);
  **Nahawand** ≈ harmonic minor; **Kurd** ≈ Phrygian; **Ajam** ≈ major.
- 12-TET approximation: Hijaz, Nahawand, Kurd and Ajam fit; Rast/Bayati/Saba need pitch bends or
  microtonal tuning (scala files, MTS-ESP, pitch-bend automation).
- Rhythms: **iqa'at** like Maqsum (4/4: Dum Tak-Tak Dum Tak), Baladi, Saidi, Ayyub (2/4), Samai (10/8).
- Turkish makam and Persian dastgah are related systems (Turkish uses 53-comma divisions).

## 8. East Asian

| Tradition | Theory |
|---|---|
| **Chinese** | pentatonic modes (gong, shang, jue, zhi, yu = the 5 rotations of C D E G A); guzheng/erhu slides; bright, ornamented |
| **Japanese** | In (Miyako-bushi), Yo, Hirajoshi, Ryukyu (C E F G B) scales; koto, shakuhachi; ma (meaningful silence) |
| **Indonesian gamelan** | slendro (≈ 5 equal steps) and pelog (7 unequal) tunings; interlocking (kotekan) parts; colotomic cycles marked by gongs |
| **Korean** | pentatonic, flexible pitch (nonghyeon vibrato), jangdan rhythmic cycles |

## 9. European folk and classical-derived

| Style | Theory |
|---|---|
| **Flamenco** | Phrygian/Andalusian cadence (Am – G – F – E), Phrygian dominant on E; compás 12-beat cycles (soleá accents 3, 6, 8, 10, 12) |
| **Celtic** | Dorian, Mixolydian, major; jigs 6/8, reels 4/4; drones (bagpipes); ornaments (cuts, rolls) |
| **Klezmer** | Freygish (Phrygian dominant), Mi Sheberach (Dorian #4); krekhts (sobbing ornaments) |
| **Balkan** | odd meters: 7/8 (2+2+3), 9/8 (2+2+2+3), 11/8; Hijaz-like modes |

## 10. Rock, metal, country, blues

| Style | Theory |
|---|---|
| **Blues** | 12-bar, I7–IV7–V7, minor pentatonic + blues scale over dominant chords, call and response, shuffle |
| **Rock** | power chords, Mixolydian (bVII), Aeolian (bVI–bVII–i), riffs from minor pentatonic |
| **Metal** | Phrygian, Locrian, harmonic minor, chromaticism, tritones, drop tunings, palm-muted 16ths |
| **Country** | major keys, I–IV–V, Mixolydian licks, pedal-steel bends, major pentatonic |
| **Funk** | one-chord vamps (dominant 9, m7), 16th-note syncopation, "the one" emphasis (James Brown), Dorian/Mixolydian |

## 11. How to learn a new style (procedure)
1. Collect 5 reference tracks; analyse each with the pipeline (tempo, key, chords, drums).
2. Make a style sheet (`learning-from-references.md`): tempo, meter/feel, mode, harmonic rhythm,
   typical progressions, drum grid, bass behaviour, melody traits, sound palette, form.
3. Identify the **non-negotiables** (dembow rhythm in reggaeton; four-on-the-floor in house; a sliding
   808 in drill) vs the variables.
4. Write 3 original 4-bar loops that keep the non-negotiables and vary everything else.
