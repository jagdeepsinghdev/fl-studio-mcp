# Harmony — function, cadences, chromaticism, modulation

Conservatory-level functional harmony (Aldwell/Schachter, Kostka/Payne, Laitz) plus the pop/modal
harmony working producers use. Chord construction lives in `chords.md`; scales in
`scales-and-modes.md`; jazz reharmonisation in `jazz-and-reharmonization.md`.

## 1. Function: tonic, predominant, dominant

| Function | Major | Minor | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tonic (T)** | I, (vi, iii as substitutes) | i, (VI, III) | home, rest |
| **Predominant / subdominant (PD/S)** | IV, ii (ii7, ii6) | iv, iiø7, VI, bII (Neapolitan) | move away, prepare the dominant |
| **Dominant (D)** | V, V7, vii°(7) | V, V7, vii°7 (raised 7th!) | tension that resolves to tonic |

The **phrase model**: T → PD → D → T. Most tonal music, from Bach to pop, is this model,
elaborated. Retrogressions (D → PD) are rarer and weaker in common practice but **normal in
rock/pop** (V → IV → I, "the rock plagal").

### Root-motion strength (Rameau/Schoenberg)
- **Down a 5th / up a 4th** (V → I, ii → V): strongest, "functional" motion.
- **Down a 3rd** (I → vi → IV → ii): smooth, shares 2 notes, a weak/gentle progression.
- **Up a step** (IV → V, V → vi): strong, used for deceptive motion.
- **Up a 3rd / down a step**: weaker, retrogressive in classical, common in rock (bVII → I, IV → iii).

## 2. Cadences (phrase endings)

| Cadence | Chords | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Perfect authentic (PAC)** | V(7) → I, both root position, tonic in the top voice | full stop |
| **Imperfect authentic (IAC)** | V → I with an inversion or the 3rd/5th on top | comma-like close |
| **Half cadence (HC)** | ends on V (… → V) | question, open |
| **Phrygian half cadence** | iv6 → V (minor; bass falls a half step) | baroque, sombre |
| **Plagal** | IV → I ("Amen") | soft, hymn, gospel; **iv → I** (minor plagal) = bittersweet, film |
| **Deceptive** | V → vi (or V → bVI) | surprise, extends the phrase |
| **Backdoor** | bVII7 → I (or iv7 → bVII7 → I) | soulful, jazz/R&B ("ii-V" from below) |
| **Aeolian / rock cadence** | bVI → bVII → i (or → I, the "Mario cadence") | epic, triumphant |
| **Andalusian** | i → bVII → bVI → V | flamenco, rock, drill/trap dark loops |

Period structure: **antecedent** (ends HC) + **consequent** (ends PAC) = a period; the
question/answer of classical phrases and of pop hooks alike.

## 3. Diatonic progressions (the "chord vocabulary")

- **Circle progression:** vi – ii – V – I (all down a fifth); minor: i – iv – VII – III – VI – iiø – V – i.
- **The axis/pop progressions:**
  - I – V – vi – IV ("four-chord song"); vi – IV – I – V (its minor-feel rotation).
  - I – vi – IV – V ('50s / doo-wop).
  - I – IV – vi – V; IV – V – iii – vi (the J-pop "royal road" IVmaj7 – V7 – iii7 – vi).
- **Minor-key loops (trap, drill, pop):** i – VI – III – VII; i – VI – VII; i – iv – VI – V;
  i – VII – VI – VII; i – bII (Phrygian).
- **Pachelbel / canon:** I – V – vi – iii – IV – I – IV – V (a descending bass sequence).
- **12-bar blues:** I7 | IV7 | I7 | I7 | IV7 | IV7 | I7 | I7 | V7 | IV7 | I7 | V7 (turnaround).
  Quick-change: bar 2 = IV7. Jazz blues adds ii-V's and a vi7.
- **Rhythm changes** (Gershwin): I – vi – ii – V ×2 … bridge III7 – VI7 – II7 – V7.

## 4. Sequences (pattern repeated at another pitch)

| Sequence | Root motion | Example in C |
|---|---|---|
| Descending fifths | −5 +4 … | C F Bdim Em Am Dm G C |
| Descending 5-6 | down a step, 5-6 motion | C Am/C … (Pachelbel-like) |
| Ascending 5-6 | up a step | C Am Dm Bdim … |
| Descending thirds | −3 | C Am F Dm |
| Ascending fifths | +5 | (retrogressive, rarer) |

Sequences are how composers modulate, build tension and write bridges. In beat-making: a melody
motif repeated a step lower each bar over a static or descending bass is a sequence.

## 5. Non-chord tones (embellishing tones)

| Tone | Approach | Leave | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passing (P) | step | step, same direction | C D E over C chord (D passing) |
| Neighbour (N) | step | step back | E F E |
| Appoggiatura (APP) | leap, on the beat | step | an accented dissonance resolving: the "sigh" |
| Escape tone (ET) | step | leap, opposite | |
| Suspension (S) | held over (prepared) | step down | 4-3, 7-6, 9-8, 2-3 (bass) |
| Retardation | held over | step **up** | 7-8 |
| Anticipation (ANT) | arrives early | stays | the final tonic a 16th early (pop vocals, trap melodies) |
| Pedal point | held (bass or top) under changing chords | | tonic pedal = home tension; dominant pedal = build-up |

Suspensions are labelled by intervals above the bass (4-3, 9-8, 7-6) and need preparation →
suspension → resolution.

## 6. Chromatic harmony

### Secondary (applied) dominants and leading-tone chords
Treat any major or minor diatonic chord as a temporary tonic and precede it by its own V:
`V/V` (D7 in C → G), `V/ii` (A7 → Dm), `V/vi` (E7 → Am), `V/IV` (C7 → F), `vii°7/V` (F#°7 → G).
Spell: root a P5 above the target, major 3rd, minor 7th. The raised note (F# in V/V) is a
temporary leading tone that **resolves up**. Pop example: I – V/vi – vi (C – E7 – Am).

### Modal mixture (borrowed chords from the parallel key)
In major, borrow from minor: **iv** (Fm in C), **bVI** (Ab), **bVII** (Bb), **bIII** (Eb),
**iiø7** (Dm7b5), **v** (Gm). These are the "emotional" pop/film chords:
- I – iv – I (minor plagal: bittersweet, "Creep", film endings)
- I – bVI – bVII – I (Mario cadence, epic)
- I – bVII – IV – I (Mixolydian rock: "Hey Jude" outro)
In minor, borrow the **Picardy third** (a major I at the end of a minor piece).

### Neapolitan (bII)
A major triad on the lowered 2nd degree, usually in first inversion (**N6** = bII6): in A minor,
Bb/D. Predominant function: N6 → V → i. Dark, operatic; trap/drill use the root-position bII as
a Phrygian loop chord (Am – Bb).

### Augmented sixth chords (predominant, resolve outward to V)
Built on b6 in the bass with #4 above, an augmented 6th apart, expanding to the octave on 5:
- **Italian (It+6):** b6 – 1 – #4 (in C: Ab C F#)
- **French (Fr+6):** b6 – 1 – 2 – #4 (Ab C D F#)
- **German (Ger+6):** b6 – 1 – b3 – #4 (Ab C Eb F#), sounds like Ab7 (enharmonic to a dominant 7:
  the pivot for modulating by a half step / to the tritone).
Film and classical climaxes; in pop it's heard as a bVI7 → V (the "tritone sub of V/V").

### Common-tone and chromatic chords
- **Common-tone diminished 7th** (ct°7): C – C#°7(with C) – C; a neighbouring embellishment.
- **Chromatic mediants:** chords a 3rd apart sharing one tone with a changed quality: C – E, C – Ab,
  C – A, C – Eb (major ↔ major). **The film-score sound** (Williams, Zimmer, Shore): heroic, magical,
  "space". Neo-Riemannian theory (P, L, R transformations) formalises these.
  - **P (parallel):** C ↔ Cm. **R (relative):** C ↔ Am. **L (leading-tone exchange):** C ↔ Em.
  - Chains: PL (C → Cm → Ab), the "hexatonic pole" C ↔ Abm (sinister, the villain chord).
- **Tritone relation:** C ↔ F# (or Gb): maximum distance; ominous.

## 7. Modulation (changing key)

| Technique | How | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Pivot (common) chord** | a chord diatonic to both keys, reinterpreted (Am = vi in C = ii in G) | smooth, classical |
| **Direct / phrase modulation** | just start the new key at a phrase boundary | pop |
| **Truck-driver / gear change** | up a semitone or whole tone for the last chorus, often via the new key's V | pop climax (cheesy if overused) |
| **Common-tone** | hold one note, change the harmony under it | film, magical |
| **Chromatic / enharmonic** | reinterpret a dim7 (4 possible resolutions) or a Ger+6 ↔ V7 | romantic, dramatic |
| **Sequential** | a sequence carries you to a new key | development sections, bridges |
| **Modal shift** | same tonic, change mode (C major → C minor) | sudden darkness/brightness |

**Closely related keys:** relative, dominant, subdominant and their relatives (one accidental
apart). Distant keys (tritone, chromatic mediants) need stronger preparation or a deliberate shock.

## 8. Modal and non-functional harmony (pop, rock, film, hip-hop)

- **Modal harmony** avoids the V → I pull: establish the tonic by **repetition and pedal**, and use
  the mode's **characteristic chord**: Dorian **IV** major (Dm – G), Mixolydian **bVII** (G – F),
  Lydian **II** major (F – G/F), Phrygian **bII** (Em – F), Aeolian **bVI – bVII**.
- **Loops (2-4 chords)** are the norm in hip-hop/trap/EDM; function comes from the loop's tension
  and the melody, not from cadences. The **drone** (one chord) is valid; motion moves into bass,
  melody and texture.
- **Planing (parallel harmony):** moving one chord shape in parallel (Debussy; house organ stabs;
  neo-soul). Diatonic planing stays in the key; chromatic planing keeps the exact shape.
- **Pedal-point harmony:** chords change over a fixed bass: film tension builds, EDM.
- **Ambiguity:** sus chords, power chords and quartal voicings avoid the 3rd, so the mode stays open.

## 9. Harmonic rhythm
How often chords change. Fast harmonic rhythm = energy, sophistication (jazz, gospel); slow = hypnotic,
space for the vocal (trap: 1-2 bars per chord or a drone; house: 1-2 bars). **Speeding the harmonic
rhythm** into a cadence (doubling chord changes in the last bar) is a classic build.

## 10. Harmonising a melody (the professional procedure)
1. Mark the **cadence points** (phrase ends) and pick cadences first (PAC, HC, deceptive).
2. On strong beats, find chords that contain the melody note (each note belongs to 3+ diatonic triads).
3. Choose the bass line next (outer-voice counterpoint: contrary motion to the melody, no
   parallel 5ths/8ves: `voice-leading-and-counterpoint.md`).
4. Fill the inner voices with smooth voice leading.
5. Add colour: substitutions, secondary dominants, borrowed chords, extensions; reharmonise
   repeated phrases differently the second time (`jazz-and-reharmonization.md`).

## 11. Dark-beat cheat sheet (minor key, producer use)

| Progression | Feel | In C# minor |
|---|---|---|
| i (drone) | hypnotic; motion in the 808/melody | C#m |
| i – VI | cinematic, the classic dark loop | C#m – A |
| i – VI – VII | lifting, heroic | C#m – A – B |
| i – VI – III – VII | emotional pop-trap | C#m – A – E – B |
| i – iv | heavy, sad | C#m – F#m |
| i – bII | menacing (Phrygian) | C#m – D |
| i – V (harmonic minor) | dramatic | C#m – G# |
| i – bVII – bVI – V | Andalusian, drill/flamenco | C#m – B – A – G# |
| VI – VII – i | rising to home | A – B – C#m |
| i – iv – i (with a Picardy I at the end) | bittersweet resolve | C#m – F#m – C# |

### Writing over a one-chord drone
- **The 808 moves:** root most of the time, walk-ups/downs into the next phrase.
- **The melody implies chords:** lean on b6 (implies VI), b7 (VII) or 4 (iv) for a beat or two.
- **Pedal point:** keep the bass on the root while upper chords change: tension without leaving home.
- **Texture changes** every 4/8 bars (`form-and-arrangement.md`).
