# Jazz harmony, chord-scale theory and reharmonisation

The harmonic toolbox of jazz, gospel, neo-soul, R&B and lo-fi (Berklee / Levine *The Jazz Theory
Book* / Mark Levine and Barry Harris practice). Chord construction: `chords.md`.

## 1. The ii–V–I (the core cadence)

| Key | ii | V | I |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major (C) | Dm7 | G7 | Cmaj7 |
| Minor (C) | Dm7b5 (iiø7) | G7b9 (or G7alt) | Cm(maj7) / Cm6 / Cm7 |

- Practise in all 12 keys around the circle of fifths. Most standards are chains of ii–V's in
  different keys.
- **Guide tones:** Dm7 (F C) → G7 (F B) → Cmaj7 (E B). The 7th of each chord falls a half step
  to the 3rd of the next.
- **Tritone in V7** (B and F in G7) = the engine of resolution.

## 2. Chord-scale theory (what scale over what chord)

| Chord | Default scale | Colour options |
|---|---|---|
| maj7 | Ionian (avoid the 4th) | Lydian (#11) for a modern/open sound |
| m7 (as ii) | Dorian | — |
| m7 (as vi / iii) | Aeolian / Phrygian | |
| m6, m(maj7) | melodic minor | |
| 7 (resolving to major) | Mixolydian | bebop dominant; Lydian dominant (7#11) |
| 7 (resolving to minor) | Phrygian dominant (b9 b13) | altered; half-whole diminished (7b9 13) |
| 7alt | altered (7th mode of melodic minor) | |
| 7sus4 | Mixolydian (4 is OK) | Dorian b2 for sus(b9) |
| m7b5 | Locrian | Locrian ♮2 (more modern) |
| dim7 | whole-half diminished | |
| 7#5 / 7b13 | whole-tone / Mixolydian b6 | |

**Avoid notes** (classically defined): a scale tone a half step above a chord tone (the 4th over
maj7 and dominant 7; the b6 over m7 when the chord is ii). Fine as passing tones, bad as held notes.

## 3. Substitution (replace a chord with one of the same function)

| Substitution | Rule | Example (key C) |
|---|---|---|
| **Diatonic (3rd-related)** | chords a 3rd apart share 2 notes | C ↔ Am ↔ Em; F ↔ Dm |
| **Tritone substitution** | replace V7 with the dominant a tritone away (same 3rd and 7th, swapped) | G7 → **Db7** → C (chromatic bass D–Db–C) |
| **Related ii** | precede any dominant with its ii | G7 → Dm7 G7 |
| **Secondary dominants** | V7 of any diatonic target | C – **A7** – Dm7 – G7 – C |
| **Backdoor dominant** | bVII7 → I (from iv7) | Fm7 – Bb7 – C |
| **Diminished passing chords** | a dim7 a half step above/below links two chords | C – **C#°7** – Dm7; Cmaj7 – C#°7 – Dm7 – D#°7 – C/E |
| **Modal interchange** | borrow from the parallel minor | Fm6, Abmaj7, Bb7, Dm7b5 in C major |
| **Coltrane changes** | major-3rd key cycle with V7's between | Cmaj7 – Eb7 – Abmaj7 – B7 – Emaj7 – G7 – C (Giant Steps) |
| **Sus substitution** | V7 → V7sus4 or IV/V (F/G) | the gospel/pop "soft dominant" |
| **Chromatic approach** | approach any chord from a half step above (same quality) | Ebm7 → Dm7 |

## 4. Reharmonisation workflow (pro method)

1. Identify the melody note on each strong beat; it must be a chord tone or a usable tension of the new chord.
2. Keep the cadences (structure); reharmonise the middle of phrases.
3. Apply, one at a time: secondary dominants → ii–V's → tritone subs → passing diminished chords →
   modal-interchange colours → chromatic approach chords.
4. Check the bass line: aim for smooth or chromatic bass motion (tritone subs create half-step bass lines).
5. Check voice leading with guide tones (§1).
6. Contrast: reharmonise only the *repeat* of a phrase so the listener notices the change.

**Constant-structure reharm:** use one chord quality for every melody note (e.g. maj7#11 or m11),
moving the root freely (Bill Evans, film, house/neo-soul pads).
**Line clichés:** a chromatic line inside a static chord: Cm – Cm(maj7) – Cm7 – Cm6 (the
"Bond/Stairway" cliché), C – Caug – C6 – C7.

## 5. Gospel, R&B and neo-soul harmony

- **Passing chords everywhere:** walk-ups (I – ii7 – iii7 – IV), diminished passing chords,
  chromatic approach chords before each target.
- **The 2-5-1 with extensions:** Dm9 – G13(b9) – Cmaj9.
- **"Gospel IV":** IV/V and IV/I (F/G, F/C) slash chords.
- **Neo-soul staples:** maj9, m9, m11, 7#9, 13sus4; quartal voicings; planed parallel chords; the
  **IVmaj7 – iii7 – vi7** loop; the "D'Angelo" chromatic slides; slightly late, laid-back chord
  placement (`rhythm.md` pocket).
- **Lo-fi:** ii9 – V13 – Imaj9 – vi9 loops, detuned/wow-flutter keys, a Dorian minor tonic (m9/m11).

## 6. Blues harmony
- The blues **I7 is a tonic dominant** (not a V); the b3/b5/b7 blue notes sit over major chords
  (major/minor 3rd clash = the blues).
- Jazz blues (in F): F7 | Bb7 | F7 | Cm7 F7 | Bb7 | B°7 | F7/A D7 | Gm7 C7 | Am7 D7 | Gm7 C7 | F7 D7 | Gm7 C7.
- Minor blues: im7 | ivm7 | im7 | im7 | ivm7 | ivm7 | im7 | im7 | bVI7 | V7alt | im7 | V7alt.

## 7. Modal jazz
- Few chords, long durations (Miles Davis "So What": 16 bars D Dorian, 8 Eb Dorian, 8 D Dorian).
- Quartal voicings (So What chord: E A D G B over D), pedal points, emphasis on the mode's colour note.
- Close cousin of trap/house loops: harmony is static; melody and texture create motion.

## 8. Improvisation vocabulary (useful for writing hooks and runs too)
- **Arpeggios / guide tones** on strong beats; **enclosures** (approach a target from above and
  below: D# – C# – D); **chromatic passing tones** (bebop); **motivic development**
  (sequence, inversion, rhythmic displacement).
- **Pentatonic superimposition:** C maj7 → G or D major pentatonic; C7alt → Eb/Gb minor pentatonic.
- **Call and response** with yourself; leave space (Miles Davis).
